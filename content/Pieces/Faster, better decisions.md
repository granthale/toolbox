In the last few weeks, I've had to make a few relatively simple decisions, most recently on whom I want to share my next apartment with. These decisions have gotten as complicated as I've let them — and in the recent case — expanded to an entire day of thinking & analyzing, much to my ire.

To avoid over-rotating in the future, my proposed decision-making algorithm:
1. Pre-work
	- Know what you want — as simple as this sounds, this is upstream of all decisions; I use [[My planning system]] to keep an eye on this
	- Write down the question
	- "Size" the opportunity (i.e., how much will this actually impact you?)
	- Identify how reversible it is (i.e., [one-way vs. two-way doors](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rxsdOQa_QkM*))
2. Time-bound based on your pre-work (e.g., roommate decision in 1-hour)
3. Do the work
	- Structure the decision (i.e., create a framework); "a problem well-stated is a problem half solved" (see [When facing a complicated problem, don't try to solve it, try to understand it](https://www.henrikkarlsson.xyz/p/problem-solving)); ultimately, [expected value](https://www.principles.com/principles/92ee4a9c-0f85-4f38-b6ed-69c676ada385/) is what you want to arrive at
	- Identify what you're confident in and what you're not; arrive at the critical questions as quickly as possible — these are the highest-leverage things to think about
	- For critical questions, [expand them](https://www.henrikkarlsson.xyz/p/writing-to-think); make them tangible (apply [fear-setting](https://tim.blog/2017/05/15/fear-setting/)); for risks, identify mitigations
4. Make the decision (positive EV) and then make the decision right; especially for low-value decisions, make them at 70-80% of information — 100% often is too expensive and too slow
5. Reflect — action creates information; keep a decision journal and record why you made the decision; reflect later on whether it was the right decision given the information you had (i.e., was it positive EV?)
6. Get on with your day!

Misc notes:
- There's a [strong case for trusting your intuition](https://x.com/maxmarchione/status/2038489906360758600) in cases when you have a high [believability index](https://www.principles.com/principles/633d5d13-8610-425f-ad62-cd62347d9165/)
- Consensus is expensive; Elon is notoriously fast at making big decisions, and it seems like part of that is about how much ownership he has over his companies; in situations where consensus is required, it can be useful to designate a leader that owns the decision
