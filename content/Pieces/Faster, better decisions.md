In the last few weeks, I've had to make a few relatively simple decisions, most recently on whom I want to share my next apartment with. These decisions have gotten as complicated as I've let them — and in the recent case — expanded to an entire day of thinking & analyzing, much to my ire.

Elon's the canonical example of someone who makes decisions and with a notorious velocity. As someone who has the tendency to over-rotate, here is how I'm planning to address decisions moving forward.

Steps:
1. Know what you want - as simple as this sounds, this is upstream of all decisions; I use [[My planning system]] to keep an eye on this
2. Write down the question
3. "Size" the opportunity and identify how reversible it is (i.e., [one-way vs. two-way doors](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rxsdOQa_QkM*)); time-bound your efforts based on this
4. Structure the decision (i.e., create a framework; step 1's framework can be used in many cases
	> "A problem well-stated is a problem half solved" (see [When facing a complicated problem, don't try to solve it, try to understand it](https://www.henrikkarlsson.xyz/p/problem-solving))
5. Identify what you're confident in and what you're not; arrive at the critical questions as quickly as possible
